# MlimaLink Launch & Marketing Plan — Mt Kenya Region

> Internal working plan. Goal: become the number one multi-vendor marketplace in the Mt Kenya region by winning town by town, with local sellers, local riders and local trust.

---

## 1. Goal and principles

**Goal:** the platform Mt Kenya shops, service providers, farmers and riders use every week to sell and deliver locally.

**Principles**

1. **Density before reach.** Win a few towns completely before spreading across the region. Delivery only works when sellers, riders and buyers are close together.
2. **Supply first.** Onboard sellers and riders before advertising to shoppers. An empty marketplace kills first impressions.
3. **Pay for results, not signups.** Mobilizers earn when the sellers they recruit actually make sales.
4. **Asset-light.** We connect local sellers and independent riders. No warehouses and no stock of our own.
5. **Build on what people already use:** M-Pesa, SMS and WhatsApp, local languages, SACCOs and chamas.

## 2. Before launch: product checklist

These must be done before any public campaign.

| #   | Item                                                                                                                                    | Why it matters                                                    |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Prices in KES** (currently USD)                                                                                                       | Traders and shoppers think in shillings                           |
| 2   | **M-Pesa payments** (Daraja STK Push, payouts to Till / Pochi la Biashara)                                                              | How people pay, and how our commission is collected automatically |
| 3   | **SMS / WhatsApp alerts** for orders, delivery codes and rider jobs                                                                     | Many users rarely check email                                     |
| 4   | **Fuller Mt Kenya town list** (Karatina, Othaya, Kerugoya/Kutus, Murang'a, Kangema, Chuka, Maua, Runyenjes, Nyahururu, Ol Kalou, Mwea…) | Users who can't find their town won't sign up                     |
| 5   | **Referral codes** linked to users, sellers and riders                                                                                  | Powers the ambassador programme and word of mouth                 |
| 6   | **Kikuyu / Kimeru / Kiembu copy** on key screens and marketing                                                                          | Builds trust with older traders                                   |
| 7   | Seller onboarding in **under 10 minutes on a phone**                                                                                    | Agents sign sellers up at market stalls                           |

Already built and worth marketing: **pay on delivery**, the **4-digit delivery code**, **rider tracking**, **discount codes**, and the **Services** listings (fundis, salons, tutors, mechanics).

## 3. Mobilizers: who brings people in

| Channel                                                                                                                                    | What they bring                                         | How we engage them                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **County governments** (trade, cooperatives and ICT departments)                                                                           | Legitimacy, market access, ward administrators, barazas | Joint "Buy from your county" launches, stands at markets and Biashara Centres, letters to permit holders |
| **Cooperatives and SACCOs** (tea/KTDA factories, coffee, dairy, SACCOs)                                                                    | Large, trusting member bases with regular income        | Member onboarding days, member-only discounts, co-branded campaigns                                      |

| **Universities and colleges** (DeKUT, Karatina, Chuka, Meru University, Kirinyaga, Murang'a, Embu, Laikipia, KeMU, Mount Kenya University) | Student ambassadors, early buyers, service providers    | Campus ambassador programme, student service listings                                                    |
| **Vernacular media** (Inooro, Kameme, Coro, Muuga, Mugambo Jwetu, local TikTok creators)                                                   | Mass reach at low cost                                  | Real trader success stories, call-in shows, market-day live coverage                                     |

### The core engine: MlimaLink Ambassadors

A paid local agent network of boda riders, students, chama leaders, and cyber café or M-Pesa agent owners.

- **Paid per activated seller**: a seller who completes their first few real orders, not a bare signup.
- **Plus a small share of that seller's sales** for the first 3–6 months, so agents keep supporting them.
- **Tools:** a personal referral code, a simple dashboard of their sellers and earnings, and printed QR flyers.
- **Tiers:** Ambassador → Town Lead → County Lead, as their network grows.

## 4. Rollout phases

### Phase 0 — Prepare (weeks 1–6)

- Finish the product checklist in section 2.
- Pick the **anchor towns**, for example Nyeri with Karatina, Embu, and Meru or Thika.
- Recruit the first 20–30 ambassadors per anchor town.
- Sign up the first county partner, 2–3 SACCOs or cooperatives, and 1–2 boda SACCOs.

### Phase 1 — Anchor towns (months 2–4)

- **Supply target per town:** 150–300 active sellers and service providers, and 30–50 riders.
- **Market-day onboarding:** a MlimaLink tent at Karatina, Kutus, Chuka or Meru markets. List traders on the spot and hand out shopper codes.
- **Then open to shoppers:** county-backed launch event, radio campaign, first-order discount codes, and a "Pay when it arrives" trust message.
- Focus categories: farm produce (avocado, macadamia, dairy, honey), agro-inputs, hardware, food, and local services.

### Phase 2 — Expand (months 5–9)

- Move to the next towns only after a town passes the **gates** in section 7.
- Copy the same playbook: county partner, then SACCOs, ambassadors, market days, and finally shopper adverts.
- Start offering the Pro seller tier and promoted listings.

### Phase 3 — Region leader (months 10–18)

- Cover all Mt Kenya counties: Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang'a, Kiambu, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Meru, Tharaka-Nithi, Embu.
- Partnerships for rider insurance, bike financing and seller stock loans.
- County analytics offering and local procurement channels.

## 5. Partnership tracks

### County governments

- **Pitch:** more local trade, youth jobs as riders, and insight into the local economy. MlimaLink complements the county's existing digital systems rather than replacing them.
- **Ask:** endorsement, market access, promotion to permit holders, and joint launch events.
- **Note:** don't request permit-holder data directly. Respect the Data Protection Act and let the county do the outreach.

### Safaricom (partner, not competitor)

- **M-Pesa via Daraja:** STK Push at checkout, seller payouts, automatic commission.
- **M-Pesa Mini App:** MlimaLink inside the M-PESA app, reaching users without an app download.
- **Startup and SME programmes:** check current programmes for funding and co-marketing.
- **Longer term:** propose MlimaLink as the marketplace and delivery layer for county businesses.

### Financial partners (SACCOs, banks, insurers)

- Seller stock loans based on sales history, bike financing for riders, rider accident and parcel insurance. We earn referral fees.

## 6. Revenue model

| Stream                            | Model                                                                                         | Priority |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| **Order commission**              | 3–8% by category, deducted automatically via M-Pesa                                           | Core     |
| **Delivery fee share**            | 10–15% of the delivery fee (the rider keeps the rest)                                         | Core     |
| **Seller subscriptions**          | Free tier, plus Pro at about KSh 500–1,500/month (more listings, better placement, analytics) | Phase 2  |
| **Promoted listings and adverts** | Category placements for agro-dealers, hardware, SACCO products                                | Phase 2  |
| **Service leads**                 | Small fee per accepted service request                                                        | Phase 2  |
| **Financial partnerships**        | Referral fees on loans, financing and insurance                                               | Phase 3  |
| **County contracts**              | Market analytics dashboards, local procurement                                                | Phase 3  |

## 7. What we measure, and when to expand

Track **per town**, weekly:

- Active sellers (at least 1 order a week)
- Monthly order value (GMV)
- Repeat buyers within 30 days
- Median delivery time
- Ambassador activation rate (recruited sellers who become active)
- Rider acceptance time (how long a posted job waits)

**Expansion gate: a town is "won" when**

- repeat-buyer rate is about 60% or higher;
- median delivery is under 2 hours;
- at least 150 sellers are active weekly;
- posted jobs are accepted within 15 minutes on average.

Signups alone are a vanity number and don't count.

---

## 8. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk                                                                   | Mitigation                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spreading too thin                                                     | Strict town gates before expanding                                               |
| Fake or dormant signups                                                | Pay ambassadors on activation, not signup                                        |
| Low buyer trust                                                        | Pay on delivery, delivery code, reviews, county endorsement                      |
| Slow or unreliable delivery                                            | Recruit riders before shoppers; boda SACCO partnerships                          |
| Competition from WhatsApp/Facebook selling                             | Make listing easier than posting in a group; delivery and trust they can't offer |
| Heavy logistics costs (a lesson from earlier rural e-commerce players) | Stay asset-light: independent riders, no inventory                               |
